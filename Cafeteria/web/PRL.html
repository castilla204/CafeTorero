Security Risks

These security risks are common to most activities and affect both kitchen staff, cleaning staff, and waiters

  • Fall of people at the same level
  • Falls of people at different levels
  • Hits
  • Cuts
  • Burns from thermal contact or liquids
  • Explosions
  • Direct or indirect electrical contacts
  • Projections and sparks
  • Falling objects during handling
  • Accidents in itinere

Hygienic risks


  • Exposure to chemical contacts during the use of cleaning products for facilities and common areas
  • Exposure to noise from the movement of crockery, cutlery, bottles, etc. and due to the chatter of customers, especially loud at peak times
  • Biological contacts when handling raw meats and products and during cleaning toilets
  • Exposure to high temperatures especially in the kitchen area. We also highlight the exposure to low temperatures since many premises in the hotel sector have cold rooms

    • Ergonomic risks


    • Overexertion due to manual handling of loads or merchandise
    • Adoption of forced postures
    • Performing repetitive movements
    • Thermal discomfort due to the heat present in some facilities

    The main possible risks that may exist in an office are

    • Order and cleanliness
    • Falls at the same level
    • Falls at different level
    • Storage
    • Blows/bumps against objects and cuts by objects
    • Electrical contacts
    • Fires and emergencies

    Psychosocial risks

    Stress related to


    • The impatience shown by many customers, both in bars and restaurants and in hotels who do not like to wait to be served
    • The existence of work peaks in different time slots that varies depending on whether it is a hotel, a restaurant or a bar
    • Shift work or unforeseen schedule changes
    • Labor instability in the sector since it is a sector with high temporary hiring
    • Role conflict, by not having well defined tasks

    Mental load


    The mental load is determined by the amount and type of information that must be handled in a job. Therefore, it can be defined as the amount of deliberate effort that we must make to achieve a specific result, based on the information available.


    Workplace violence


    The concept of violence goes beyond mere physical aggression (hitting, pushing, hitting,) and includes and includes other behaviors such as:

    • Threatening verbal or physical behavior
    • Intimidating acts
    • Abusive and accusatory behaviors
